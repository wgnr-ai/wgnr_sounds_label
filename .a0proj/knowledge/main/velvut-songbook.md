## Velvut house sonic identity (synthesis of written style notes)

- **Core:** nu-alt rock / alt-metal influences, minor key, powerful baritone vocal (upfront), deep bass, full drum kit, explosive chorus lift.
- **Recurring production tags:** analog tape saturation, room reverb, wailing guitars, tight rhythm guitars.
- **Dynamics convention:** quiet verses / wall-of-sound choruses; halftime/tom-tom grooves; melodic-rap bridges used twice (Glass Horizon, Phantom Walls).
- **Documented tempos:** 92 BPM (Phantom Walls), 148 BPM (Who's Calling Me?). Keys: none documented anywhere.
- **NOTE (2026-08-31, Fault Lines session):** Principal rejected a "90s metal" demo and approved a modern-2026 tag set that DROPS tape saturation/plate reverb/palm-muted chugs/stacked power chords.
- **RESOLUTION TREND (2026-09-01):** "Who's Calling Me?" demo WITH spring reverb + tape saturation + palm-muted downstrokes in the set was rated "a hit" by the Principal — vault-family tags can read modern in the right combination. Ruling: the era test is empirical per song (pre-register markers, one-variable retries); no blanket ban either way.

## Hook conventions (reuse patterns)

1. Question-chorus (title asked twice): Are We Out of Time?, Is This How It Ends?
2. Repetition ladder + parenthetical ad-libs: Bad
3. Imperative shout + chant outro: Glass Horizon ("Break it down! / Tear it down!")
4. Title refrain: These Phantom Walls, Nothing To Me, Maybe We'll Meet Again Someday
5. Post-2026-08-31 addition (Fault Lines): stomp-chant on title phrase — "FAULT! (stomp-stomp) LINES!"
6. Question hook ×4 + conceit exit line (Who's Calling Me?) — hook asked twice per quatrain across two quatrains; the machine line ("Leave your message at the tone") as the cold closer.

## Co-Writing Workflow (proven 2026-09-01, "Who's Calling Me?")

- **Division:** the Principal writes the spine and makes every call. The team treats his sections (editorial read, drop-in swaps, honest verdicts), drafts 2-3 continuation options per open section (genuinely different, anchored to his inventory), and supplies structural notes. Complete-section delivery always — never diffs.
- **Evidence:** generation-from-scratch failed Principal review 3 consecutive times (Fault Lines, 2026-08-31); the co-writing loop produced a Principal-approved "hit" in one session. Do not revert to from-scratch generation for Velvut/Wágner lyrics.
- **Initiative growth (Principal directive 2026-09-01):** sessions compound into team style-learning — propose treatments unprompted, bring more options per pass, flag collisions/structural issues earlier. The spine and every final call remain the Principal's.

## Catalog coherence notes

- Walls/confinement imagery recurs: These Phantom Walls (passive entrapment) and Fault Lines (active rupture/reclamation) — same neighborhood, distinct angle. Keep future wall/crack themes differentiated the same way.
- Question hooks are a Velvut signature ("Are we out of time?", "Is this how it ends?", Fault Lines' "Can you feel it coming?").
- Clock-tick imagery existed in Phantom Walls; the Principal ruled tick-gimmicks "cheesy" for Fault Lines (2026-08-31) — avoid tick/countdown motifs going forward.
