## 0. Four rulings — the delivered version had drifted from the vault

The earlier delivery and the vault disagreed in four places. Vault file: `hate-me.md`, `status: draft`, `last_updated: 2026-09-22T19:26:00` — **later than the delivery**. The Principal deferred all four decisions; rulings below.

| # | Divergence | Earlier delivery | Vault | **Ruling** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Spelling | `S.O.S.` | `SOS` | **No periods.** Vault form, all five occurrences. |
| 2 | Tag counts | 2 per chorus; final chorus 2 blocks | 3 per chorus; final chorus **3 blocks (2-1-3)** | **Vault wins.** The taper is deliberate. |
| 3 | Inline directions | kept in lyrics | present in vault | **Moved to style prompt** — consistent with the other 12 docs. |
| 4 | `gang vocals` | included | included | **Stays.** Documented, and unique to this song. |

**Why the tag counts matter most.** The vault's final chorus does not simply repeat three times. It runs **two tags, then one, then three**. That middle single-tag block is a breath — the chorus steps back before it lands. Collapsing it to two blocks removes the only dynamic variation in a song that is otherwise five identical choruses.

### One contradiction found and corrected

The earlier exclude list contained **`spring reverb`**. The vault's own style notes list **`spring reverb`** as a documented element. Excluding an element the source requires would have suppressed a documented instruction. **Removed from the exclusions; carried in the style prompt instead.**

### The `gang vocals` conflict — resolved series-wide

`gang vocals` appears in exactly one Velvut file: this one (verified by grep across all 13). It is documented, so it wins on its own song.

But it was **excluded by name** in Are We Out of Time? and Who's Calling Me?. Those two exclusions were wrong as a blanket rule and should be read as scoped: those songs have no gang-vocal instruction, this one does. **The general rule going forward: never exclude an element the song's own style notes document.**

## 2. Style prompt — paste into Style of Music

```
dark industrial synth-rock, electro-industrial, alt-metal edge, heavy, driving, 148 BPM, predominantly minor key, desperate, cornered, self-lacerating, distorted synth bass, gated sequenced saw-bass pulses, detuned saw stack stabs, hard analog synth pads, aggressive mono-synth lead with portamento, metallic industrial percussion hits, tom-tom-groove verses, programmed kick with weight, tape saturation, parallel compression, quiet-loud dynamics, pre-chorus lift, wall-of-sound choruses, gang vocals on the hook, sub-bass weight, spring reverb, low and deep baritone male vocal upfront, distorted pre-chorus vocal delivery, shouted pre-chorus count-in, gritty delivery, memorable chorus hook, hard cut ending
```

### The `[Distorted vocals]` / `[Shouting]` pre-chorus

The pre-chorus is not sung. It is `One, two, three` **distorted**, then `Here it comes!` **shouted**, three times across the song.

Both are carried as style instructions: `distorted pre-chorus vocal delivery` and `shouted pre-chorus count-in`. **This is the one place in the Velvut catalogue where a raised voice is documented** — which is why `screaming vocals` and `harsh vocals` are *not* excluded here, unlike every other song in the series. A shouted count-in is not a scream, and banning screams must not suppress the instruction.

### `gang vocals` on the hook

Documented. The `Hate me / Hate me / Hate me` tag is the natural site — it is the only repeated phrase that invites a group. Carried explicitly as `gang vocals on the hook`.

**This is the differentiating feature of the song.** Without it, Hate Me is structurally identical to Who's Calling Me?: same 148 BPM, same `predominantly minor key`, same style-note block.

### `anthemic chorus` — same ruling as Who's Calling Me?

Documented here too. Carried as `wall-of-sound choruses` for the size, with the victory-words excluded (`triumphant`, `victorious`, `stadium`, `heroic`). Hate Me is not triumphant — it is self-lacerating. The chorus is big, not victorious.

### The 148 BPM consequence

Fast, industrial, sub-bass and gang vocals is the closest the series gets to an EBM stomp. `quiet-loud dynamics` and `pre-chorus lift` are both documented and both are the guard against a club record.

### Vocal handling

`low and deep baritone vocals`. F2 floor. At 148 BPM the chorus lines are short and repeated — no climb required. The risk is the **pre-chorus**: shouting pushes a baritone up the range. Keep `Here it comes!` at speech pitch, not sung pitch. Above C#4 it stops being a shout and becomes a strain.

### Structural flags — preserved as written

1. **The final chorus is one label with three blocks, not three sections.** Preserved as a single `[Final Chorus]` containing three passes. Separating them into three labels would let Suno treat each as a new section and reset the energy — the taper depends on them being continuous.

## 5. Arrangement notes

### Five choruses, and only the last one changes shape

| Pass | Position | Arrangement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | After pre-chorus 1 | Full stack, gang vocal enters on the tag. Three tags. |
| 2 | After pre-chorus 2 | Heaviest. Bridge follows immediately. Three tags. |
| 3 (block 1 of final) | After bridge | Full. **Two tags — the taper begins.** |
| 4 (block 2 of final) | — | **One tag. The pull-back.** Gang vocal drops to the lead alone. |
| 5 (block 3 of final) | End | **Three tags. Everything back, then cut.** |

**This is the only song in the series where the final chorus has internal shape.** Everything else either builds to the end or ends flat. Hate Me steps back once and then lands.

### The pre-chorus is the only shouted moment

Three lines: `One, two, three` distorted, `Here it comes!` shouted. It appears twice.

**In the rebuild:** distort the count-in with a bit-crusher or a hard-clipped send, and shout the pickup at speech pitch. The whole gesture lasts a bar. Do not extend it, and do not carry the shout into the chorus — the contrast is the point.

### Gang vocals — five people, not a stadium

The tag `Hate me / Hate me / Hate me` is where the group enters. Two rules:

- **It is a gang, not a crowd.** Same room, close, slightly out of time. Not a choir, not a stadium chant — both are excluded.
- **It drops out in the middle final-chorus block.** The single-tag block should be the lead alone. That is what makes the third block feel like a return.

### Ending

No outro. The last line is the third `Hate me` of the final block. **Cut dead on it.** No fade, no ring-out, no resolve.

## 9. Audition notes

1. **Victory test — the primary judgement call.** If the chorus sounds like a triumph, it has failed. The lyric is self-hatred — `I know you hate me` is the narrator agreeing. Listen for a resolve, a rising key change, a heroic lift.
2. **Pre-chorus test.** `One, two, three` distorted, `Here it comes!` shouted, and then *gone*. If it comes back sung, the delivery instruction was swallowed by Style Influence — drop it, don't raise it.
3. **Gang vocal test.** Close and human on the tag, absent in the middle final-chorus block. A choir or a stadium chant is a fail; so is a gang vocal that carries through all three blocks.
4. **The taper.** Two tags, then one, then three. If all three final blocks are identical, the song's only internal variation is gone — likely, and that is the rebuild's job.
5. **Tempo.** 148. Fast and forward. If it drags, darkwave defaults are the cause.
6. **Spelling.** If a render produces `S.O.S.` in the sung output, that is Suno's punctuation handling, not a lyric change — but the filed lyric is `SOS`.
7. **Ending.** Hard cut on the third `Hate me`. No fade, no ring-out.
